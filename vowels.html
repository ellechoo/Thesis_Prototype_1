How to read this chart

Up and down is F1, which follows how open your mouth is. “Heed” sits high (mouth nearly closed); “had” and “hod” sit low (mouth open).

Left and right is F2, which follows where your tongue is. Front vowels like “heed” sit left, back vowels like “who'd” sit right.

Accents show up as shifts. Many Californians say “who'd” further forward (left). Many Americans say “hod” and “hawed” in the same spot (the cot–caught merger). Southern British speakers say “had” lower and further back than most Americans.

The reference is a 1950s General American average, not a “correct” target. It's a fixed point to compare against. Higher-pitched voices make formants harder to measure, so expect more scatter.
