Why this passage?

Everyone reads the same words
So the archive compares voices, not sentences. When two portraits differ, it's because of how two people speak, not what they said. The accent check listens to sound rather than words, and the same text keeps that comparison fair.
It holds every main English vowel
From the “ee” in she and the “i” in milk to the “oo” in poodle and the “ur” in fur. Your vowels, and how you shape each one, are among the clearest clues to where you learned English. They become the spots on your portrait and the points on your vowel chart.
Its length is known
It has exactly 42 syllables, so dividing by your reading time gives your speaking rate.
It's plain and quick
Everyday words, nothing hard to pronounce, and under ten seconds to read. Researchers who collect accents work the same way: the Speech Accent Archive has every speaker read one shared paragraph.

Momo is a small poodle and she is the color of milk chocolate. She has fluffy fur, a good nose for snacks, and she barks at every red truck that passes our house.
